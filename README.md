# The Bunny Man

## Overview

The Bunny Man is a Northern Virginia urban legend documented in detail by Brian A. Conley, historian-archivist of the Fairfax County Public Library. It originates in two genuine and police-reported incidents in October 1970. On the evening of 19 October 1970, U.S. Air Force Academy cadet Robert Bennett and his fiancée were sitting in a car parked on Guinea Road in the Burke area of Fairfax County when a figure approached, complained that they were trespassing, and threw a hatchet through the front passenger window. Bennett described the figure as wearing 'a white suit with long bunny ears'; his fiancée described a white outfit but was unsure about the ears. Ten days later, on 30 October 1970, construction security guard Paul Phillips encountered a person in a 'gray, black, and white bunny costume' on the porch of an unfinished home in Kings Park West, also off Guinea Road. The figure attacked a porch post with a long-handled axe and shouted at Phillips to leave. Police investigated but never identified a suspect. From these two incidents, an elaborate folkloric tradition grew up around the Colchester Road railroad overpass near Clifton, Virginia ('Bunny Man Bridge'), incorporating false backstories about an 1904 escaped asylum patient (the Lorton Reformatory was not in operation at that date), Halloween rituals, and chains of Halloween-night murders. A 1973 University of Maryland student paper documented 54 distinct legend variants. Conley, after extensive archival research, has emphasised that the bridge has no documented connection to the 1970 events themselves.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Haunted bridge / vengeful spirit** _(Castle of Spirits (1999) and successor websites, Local legend-trippers)_

Pop-folkloric framing: the Bunny Man is a ghost or supernatural entity haunting the Colchester Overpass, especially active on Halloween night.

### Skeptical

**Two real incidents, mythologised** _(Brian A. Conley, Fairfax County Public Library)_

The factual core consists of two confirmed October 1970 incidents involving a costumed assailant; everything beyond — the asylum escapee, the hangings from the bridge, the Halloween-night murders — is post-hoc folklore that began circulating in print and online from the 1990s onward.

**Costumed prankster or disturbed individual** _(Fairfax County Police investigators)_

The most parsimonious reading of the original 1970 incidents is a single individual, possibly a college-age prankster or a person experiencing mental illness, never identified by police.
